Murmuration as a field

No birds are drawn. The flock is a finite body of density carried backwards through time along a field of drifting vortices, so its deformation compounds — it folds over itself rather than wobbling.

Too stretchy, too fast

  • The strain compounds, so a modest number was not one. The shear waves are what draw the body out, and their effect multiplies over a whole material lifetime — the amplitude in there was pulling a circle out to roughly six times its length before the material was replaced. It is down by well over half, and the flock keeps a body now rather than resolving into ribbons within a couple of seconds.
  • Churn controls it. It used to scale only the vortices, which was the smaller half of the stretching. It scales the shear waves too now, so it is the stretchiness dial — push it up for the drawn-out version.
  • Two corners smoothed. The foreshortening ran on the absolute value of a sine, which has a kink at every zero crossing — precisely the moment the body is fully edge-on, so it stopped narrowing and began widening with a jolt in the rate. It is a squared sine now: same range, same period, no corner. And the changeover phase field is measured on the rigid body axes, so a startle no longer stretches the phase field itself and shifts which regions are due to hand over.

Before that: crossfading between configurations

Still jumping — and it looked like a dissolve between two unrelated arrangements rather than one arrangement moving.

  • It was doing exactly that, by construction. The material has a lifetime, so generations have to hand over — and with only two of them, half a lifetime apart, every handover was between a nearly-fresh arrangement and a nearly-shredded one. Disparate is the word. Worse, the gentle raised-cosine weighting gave a barely-deformed newcomer half the picture while it was still a smooth blob.
  • Five generations, weighted much more sharply. One holds most of the picture most of the time, and the two that ever share it are a fifth of a lifetime apart instead of half of one — near-identical arrangements a moment apart, so the handover reads as a slight softening rather than as a dissolve.
  • And they no longer all turn over at once. The changeover phase is offset smoothly across the body, so a handover is local churn while its neighbours hold. A single global phase meant the entire flock crossfaded in unison, which is most of why it was legible as a crossfade at all.
  • Cheaper than the two it replaces. A generation's weight is floored to zero smoothly rather than cut off at a threshold, so one that is not worth drawing can simply not be traced — with nothing popping when it goes.

Before that: stutters into a different shape

  • The startle was a step. A strike set the contraction to full in a single frame. That was harmless while it only scaled the outline — but it now sits inside the body frame, where it is applied to the traced material coordinate, so a strike was instantaneously remapping the texture and the boundary together. Every few seconds the whole flock jumped to a different shape between one frame and the next. It has an attack now: fast, and finite. Birds are quick; they are not discontinuous.
  • A late frame here is worse than a dropped one. The flock is a function of time, so a frame that arrives late does not repeat — it shows a moment that has already moved on, which reads as a lurch. The tracing got expensive enough that a phone can fall behind, so the render resolution now backs off until the frame rate holds, and climbs back when it can.
  • Not the boundary of the traced region, which was the other suspect — an arm crossing it would be cut off with a straight edge and pop. Measured against a bound twice as generous, the average mass on screen is the same to within a per cent. It is measured on the rigid body axes now rather than the foreshortened ones, which had made it tightest exactly when the body was thinnest.

Before that: the outline never changed

Not literally an outline, but it was still clear where the flock started and ended, and that never changed.

  • The boundary was a stencil. The interior folded; the edge was a rigid oval that the folding happened inside. It rotated and narrowed, and the rim was ragged, but its shape was decided in advance and the flow never touched it. Where the flock ends is now worked out the same way as everything else — from the traced material coordinate — so the boundary is made of the same stuff as the interior and folds with it. Arms get drawn off it, notches fold into it, pieces tear away and drift.
  • The seed is just an oval, and that is fine. It used to carry a two-scale ragged wobble, which is now pointless: an oval that has been through two seconds of compounding strain is more irregular than any wobble could make it, and irregular in ways that mean something.
  • The shear had to stop being uniform. A uniform shear is a linear map, and a linear map can only ever turn an oval into a longer oval — so however hard it was stretched, the body kept resolving into a smooth sliver with straight flanks. It is now two crossed shear waves, each with velocity perpendicular to the direction it varies in, which makes them divergence-free: they stretch and fold without bunching material up. Crossed waves like these are the standard recipe for chaotic mixing.
  • Half the tracing was wasted. Eight steps regardless of age meant a newborn generation paid full price to travel almost no distance. The step size is fixed now and the count follows the age — and since the two ages always sum to one period, the pair costs what one used to.

Before that: a flag with a pattern on it

  • Nothing accumulated. The trace stepped five times through the flow at a single instant — a fixed distortion applied to a fixed texture. The distortion drifted, so the picture wobbled, but no part of the pattern ever went anywhere. That is a flag, exactly. The trace now runs backwards through time: each point is followed through the flow as it was one second ago, two seconds ago, and the deformation compounds along the way. Compounding strain is what draws a blob into a filament and then folds the filament over itself.
  • So the material has a lifetime. Strain that compounds forever tears any texture to threads, and a real flock keeps making new structure rather than stretching one arrangement indefinitely. Two generations run half a period apart; each is born undeformed at zero weight and dies fully drawn out at zero weight, so what is on screen is always the middle of a fold and the changeover never shows.
  • The flow was a conveyor belt. It carried a uniform stream along the heading, nine times faster than the body itself travels. Over a long trace that just pours material in one end and out the other. Travelling is the body's job; the flow is now circulation plus a shear along the heading — speed proportional to how far across you are, which is what draws everything out into slanted filaments.
  • And it was all far too slow. Every clock in the flow — vortex drift, vortex reversal — ran on twenty to thirty-five seconds. The interior had no time to rearrange between one look and the next. They run on a few seconds now, and the vortices orbit inside the body rather than out beyond its ends.
  • The inside was a solid sheet. The specks only ever bit at the rim, so the body's interior was continuous — and a continuous sheet that folds is goo, whatever it is notionally made of. The grain now opens up throughout, so there is sky visible through the mass everywhere, not just at the edges.

Fixed in earlier passes

  • Everything is read in the body's own frame, so the interior travels, banks and compresses with the flock instead of the flock being a hole sliding across fixed wallpaper — which is what made it read as growing and shrinking.
  • The body is smaller than the frame, deliberately. You cannot see a thing turn if you never see the ends of it. Turning edge-on now goes narrow and dark together, because the same birds stack.
  • The edge thins rather than fades — birds are removed by a rising threshold and the ones that remain stay solid, instead of every one of them going half-transparent, which read as a blurry lens.
  • Round specks, rotated octaves, tapering ribbons. The noise had a grid inside it and the specks were axis-aligned rectangles, which is what looked squared-off.
  • Strike sends an agitation wave — a ripple racing through far faster than the body travels, with the mass balling up behind it. It fires on its own at irregular intervals; on a fixed clock it read as breathing rather than as a startle.

Churn is vortex strength — how violently it folds. Tempo is the single clock everything runs on. It used to scale the flow and the clock the flow is measured against, so it changed speed quadratically; it does one thing now. The default is a good deal slower than it was — say if it wants to be slower still. Grain is the illusion of number, and also sets how coarse the stragglers at the edge are.
